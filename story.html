One quota family, two snapshots

The Netherlands, 2025, skates and rays in the North Sea. What the regulated-stock family looked like on 12 February, what it looked like on 13 August, and exactly where the difference sits.

What a family is

A parent quota is a legal ceiling. Underneath it sit its special conditions: species that report their own share without a ceiling of their own, and area conditions that carry a sub-limit and may have children in turn. Every tonne caught by a child is also counted against the parent.

On 12 February the SRX / 2AC4-C family for the Netherlands held 671.748 tonnes: 0.056 caught directly against the parent, the rest arriving from four children below it.

What moved

Six months later the family holds 681.148 tonnes, 9.400 more. The boxes in red are the ones whose own figure changed. Two of the four children did not move at all.

Where it went

Almost all of the 9.400 tonnes came up through the area condition. RJC caught 9.400 tonnes more in *07D2., that lifted the area condition from 62.400 to 71.800, and the parent inherited it unchanged.

The other two movements cancel: RJC / 2AC4-C rose by 0.036 and the parent's own catches fell by 0.036. That is a re-attribution between two lines of the same family, not new fish.

How we know

The reported data is copied every day it changes. Fifteen such copies exist between February and August; the two shown here are the first and the last. Every family is rebuilt from each copy in the same way, so a difference between two dates is a difference in the declarations, not in the method.

Within a family, each declaration is counted once, on one row, whatever level of the family it was written against. That is what lets the parent's total equal the sum of its children plus what was fished directly, to the kilogram, on every snapshot.
